The exhaustion of always being the strong one

By Valessa Rizzotto, MA, RCC, CCC · April 2026

There is a role some people take on so deeply that, over time, it can become difficult to notice that it is a role at all. They are the person others turn to, the one who stays steady during difficult moments, who keeps going when others feel overwhelmed, and who often responds to "How are you?" by focusing on everyone else.

Being that person can come with a particular kind of exhaustion. It can also come with a sense of loneliness that is difficult to explain. You may be surrounded by people who appreciate and depend on you, yet still feel that there is little space for your own needs and experiences.

For some people, being strong became part of how they learned to navigate relationships. It may have developed from early experiences where being dependable, self-sufficient, or emotionally contained felt important. Over time, caring for others may have become more familiar than allowing others to care for you.

The cost of always being the one who holds things together can be significant. When you are used to supporting others, receiving support yourself may feel unfamiliar. When people have come to see you as the one who is always capable, they may not realize when you need care too. And when asking for help feels uncomfortable or uncertain, important parts of your own emotional experience may remain unspoken.

There may also be feelings underneath the selflessness that are difficult to acknowledge, including frustration or resentment. Not because you do not genuinely care about others, but because giving can sometimes become easier than receiving.

Exploring these patterns is not about changing your caring nature or becoming less generous. It is about creating more space for your own needs, emotions, and experiences as well. You are allowed to need support and to discover what it feels like to be cared for, not only to be the person who cares for others.

Therapy can offer a space to explore how these patterns developed, what they have protected you from, and what new possibilities may become available when you no longer have to carry everything alone.
