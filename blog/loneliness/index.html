You can be surrounded by people and still feel profoundly alone

By Valessa Rizzotto, MA, RCC, CCC · March 2026

Loneliness is often associated with being alone. But many people experience loneliness while surrounded by others. They have partners, friends, colleagues, and family. They are connected to people in many ways, and yet there can still be a quiet sense of disconnection that relationships alone do not seem to reach.

This is a particular kind of loneliness: the experience of not feeling truly known.

Being liked is not the same as being known. Being present with others is not always the same as feeling seen. You can be surrounded by people who appreciate your company and still carry the feeling that only certain parts of you are visible. The parts that feel more uncertain, vulnerable, or complicated may remain hidden.

This experience can develop for many reasons. For some people, it may be connected to early experiences where certain parts of themselves did not feel fully accepted or understood. For others, adapting to the expectations of others may have become so familiar that it becomes difficult to recognize where the adaptation ends and the more personal parts of themselves begin. And sometimes, it reflects the reality that genuine intimacy takes time, trust, and a willingness to be vulnerable.

Whatever its origins, this experience deserves attention and understanding. The wish to be known and accepted as we are is a deeply human longing. It is not a sign that something is wrong with you or that your needs are too much.

Therapy can offer a space where different parts of yourself can be explored and where you can experience what it feels like to be understood without needing to present only the parts that feel easier to share.

Being truly known is not too much to want.
